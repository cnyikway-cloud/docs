RS-485 Wiring and Termination Guide for Industrial PCs

RS-485 buses on industrial PCs should be wired as a daisy chain with twisted-pair cable and 120-ohm termination at both physical ends. This guide covers pinout, termination, fail-safe biasing and the mistakes that cause most field failures.

Quick answer

Wire RS-485 as a daisy chain (device to device, no stubs longer than ~30 cm), use twisted-pair cable, and install a 120 Ω termination resistor at both physical ends of the bus only. Star or tree wiring without termination is the single most common cause of intermittent communication loss on factory floors.

On DB-9 style COM ports the typical mapping is pin 3 = A, pin 5 = B or GND depending on the adapter — always check the board manual, because miswired A/B is the second most common field fault. Swapping them usually shows up as "no response" rather than corrupted data.

Termination and biasing rules

  • Install 120 Ω (or 100–130 Ω matching your cable impedance) between A and B at both ends only — never at every device.
  • Many Yikway COM ports include a DIP-switch selectable terminator; leave it OFF on middle nodes.
  • On long or heavily loaded buses, enable fail-safe biasing at one end to hold the idle state.
  • Keep total bus length within the classical limits: ~1,200 m at 9.6–19.2 kbps; shorten proportionally at higher baud rates.
  • Route the bus away from VFD/motor power cables; if crossing is unavoidable, cross at 90°.

Mistakes we see most often

  1. Star wiring from the PC to each device — reflections and standing waves corrupt data.
  2. Terminators installed on every node — overloads the drivers and shortens effective range.
  3. Ground loop through the GND wire when devices are on different panels — use isolation. Yikway IPCs offer optional isolated COM modules for exactly this reason.
  4. Using untwisted cable next to power lines — induced noise appears as CRC errors.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the maximum RS-485 cable length on an industrial PC?

With proper termination and baud rates up to 19.2 kbps, RS-485 reliably reaches about 1,200 meters. Higher baud rates reduce the practical length; at 115.2 kbps plan for roughly 100 to 300 meters depending on cable quality.

Should every RS-485 device have a 120-ohm terminator?

No. Only the two physical ends of the bus should be terminated. Terminating every node loads the differential drivers, reduces signal amplitude and shortens the usable bus length.

Can I connect multiple industrial PCs on the same RS-485 bus?

Yes, RS-485 is a multi-drop bus that supports up to 32 unit loads (more with modern transceivers). All nodes share one pair of wires in a daisy chain, and only one node may drive the bus at a time.
