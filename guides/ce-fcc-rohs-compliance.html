CE / FCC / RoHS Compliance Path for Custom Industrial Computers

What a custom industrial PC needs to ship legally in the EU and US: which directives apply, which standards the lab tests, typical timelines and costs, and which documents the manufacturer must keep on file.

Quick answer

For the EU: a custom industrial PC falls under the EMC Directive 2014/30/EU and, with an AC adapter or mains supply, the Low Voltage Directive 2014/35/EU, plus RoHS 2011/65/EU. For the US: FCC Part 15 Subpart B (unintentional radiator, digital device). None of these require a notified body for this product class — the manufacturer tests, assembles the technical file and self-declares. Budget roughly 3–5 weeks and a few thousand dollars for a full custom model, less for a variant of an already-certified platform.

The CE path (EU), step by step

  1. Identify applicable legislation: EMC + LVD (if mains-connected) + RoHS; machinery directive only if the PC is sold as part of a machine you also supply.
  2. EMC testing at an accredited lab: emissions to EN 55032 (Class A for industrial environments), immunity to EN 55035 (ESD, EFT, surge, RF fields).
  3. LVD electrical safety: EN 62368-1 (with mains PSU) — often covered by using a certified external adapter.
  4. RoHS: gather material declarations from every component supplier; the lab or your team assembles the declaration.
  5. Technical file + EU Declaration of Conformity (DoC): test reports, BOM declarations, DoC listing standards and signatory. Keep it for 10 years.
  6. Mark CE on the product and ship. No yearly certificate renewal, but re-test when hardware changes materially.

The FCC path (US)

Digital electronics without radio: FCC Part 15 Subpart B, Class A (industrial/commercial) — verification or Supplier's Declaration of Conformity, tested to ANSI C63.4. If the unit contains Wi-Fi/5G modules, the modules carry their own grants and the host needs additional evaluation. FCC does not require CE-style marking; the compliance statement goes in the manual.

Documents a buyer should ask any manufacturer for

  • CE Declaration of Conformity (naming the exact standards and versions)
  • EMC test reports from the lab (with the tested configuration)
  • RoHS declaration and supplier material declarations
  • FCC compliance statement / test report

Frequently Asked Questions

Does CE certification for an industrial PC require a notified body?

No. Industrial PCs are self-declarable: the manufacturer tests against harmonized standards (EN 55032, EN 55035, EN 62368-1), assembles the technical file and signs the EU Declaration of Conformity. A notified body is only needed for special cases outside the normal directives.

How long does CE + FCC testing take for a custom industrial computer?

Typically 3 to 5 weeks end to end for a new model: lab scheduling, one to two weeks of testing across EMC (and safety if mains-powered), plus report and declaration preparation. Variants of an already-certified platform often need only delta testing.

Do I need new CE testing if I only change the CPU or add a card?

Minor changes on the same certified platform usually require only a documented assessment and possibly delta EMC testing; a material hardware change (new board, new PSU, new enclosure) means re-testing. Ask the manufacturer how the change affects the technical file — a competent one will answer specifically.
